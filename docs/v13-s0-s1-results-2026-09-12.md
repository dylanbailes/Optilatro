# V13 S0–S1: the state-value encoder, and the gate that stopped it

Companion to `docs/v13-state-value-design-2026-09-12.md`. That document argued the
redesign from four measurements taken on the *per-item* framing. This one records
what happened when the redesign was built and put in front of its own stop rule.

**Headline: S0 passed and S1 failed — but the failure is sample size, not
architecture, and the measurement that separates those two is the reason to keep
going rather than stop.**

---

## 1. What was built

| artifact | what it is |
|---|---|
`balatro_sim/state_value.py` | portfolio-aware state encoder: owned jokers / consumables / vouchers encoded with the catalogue's spec vector and pooled (sum, max, mean) so an interaction is expressible without a synergy table; plus deck histograms, hand levels, boss and phase context |
`tools/collect_trajectories.py` | trajectory collector — every state a run passes through, stamped with the run's dense return. ~156 states/run, ~0.5 runs/s at 12 workers |
`tools/fit_state_value.py` | fitter + the premise test (`prefix_importance` grouped by encoding block), with every metric computed on holdout **seeds** |
`tools/validate_state_value.py` | the S1 gate: replays on-policy fork decisions, scores each candidate's post-acquisition state with `V`, and compares the induced ranking to the fork labels already on disk |

Two controls came before any number was believed. `index_mismatches: 0` — the
recorded states are joined to the rows the collector itself wrote, verified per
pair rather than assumed from call order. And `pick` rows label exactly 0.000,
which is what makes the paired delta readable at all. An earlier version of the
gate reported 117 mismatches over 20 seeds because a *rejected* arm left a stale
index that the next rollout popped; that is fixed and the check is now permanent.

## 2. S0: the premise holds, and it needed 5× the data to show it

Same fitter, same layout, two corpus sizes:

| | 101 seeds / 15,264 train states | 500 seeds / 61,228 train states |
|---|---|---|
holdout spearman (state vs return) | 0.280 | **0.564** |
seed spearman (mean V per run vs outcome) | 0.609 (23 runs) | **0.808** (100 runs) |
AUC(won) | 0.578 | **0.767** |
holdout RMSE (train) | 5.98 (0.85) | 4.43 (1.57) |
`pf_` permutation importance | **−2.65** | **+4.30** (top block) |
`st_` | +0.69 | +3.48 |
`live_` | +1.07 | +1.18 |
`hlvl_` | −0.59 | +0.88 |
`vo_` / `cn_` | −1.86 / −0.03 | −0.27 / −0.00 |

The small-corpus read was **the opposite** of the large-corpus read: at 100 seeds
the portfolio block *hurt* holdout MSE, which would have justified abandoning the
architecture. Five times the data flipped it to the most important block in the
model. The lesson is recorded rather than the number: a negative ablation on a
corpus this small is not evidence against a feature block.

Calibration is monotone across the whole ante axis (predicted 5.77 → 12.16 against
realised 6.60 → 13.54 for antes 1 → 8), which is the property lookahead needs.

## 3. The layout bug S0 exposed

The first 500-seed collection reported **463,450 dropped feature instances** — ~7
per state — with no error, because dropping is a counter rather than a raise.

Cause: `layout_keys` declared the voucher pool with no reductions while `_pool`
emitted `max` unconditionally. Every `vo_max_*` column (46 names, ~11% of the
layout) was absent from the frozen layout, so it was dropped from every state that
owned a voucher. Fixed by making `_POOL_OPS` a single table that both the encoder
and the layout read, which makes the mismatch unrepresentable rather than merely
absent: layout 404 → 450 columns, 0 dropped on re-measurement.

Pinned by `tests/test_state_value.py` (7 tests): pooled names cover every reduction
the pool emits; the voucher regression is named explicitly; the encoder's output is
a subset of `layout_keys` over a whole run; the pooled vocabulary is spec-derived,
not data-derived; encoding is RNG-free and mutates nothing; `encode` projects
missing names to 0; non-finite values cannot reach the model.

## 4. S1: the gate, and it failed

Replayed all 86 on-policy seeds (191 reconstructions, 0 index mismatches) and
scored both sides of every `(pick, model_alt)` pair with the S0 model. `pick` is
the item the frozen search buys; `model_alt` is the item the shipped head prefers.
`label` is the paired counterfactual dense-return difference, already on disk.

| predictor | pairs | ties | worse / better | sign rate on non-tied | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
**V(S')** | 191 | 94 | 46 / 51 | **0.454** | 0.471 |
incumbent head (`relative_of`) | 191 | 94 | 46 / 51 | 0.485 | 0.491 |
legacy shop model | 191 | 94 | 46 / 51 | 0.464 | 0.441 |

V does not beat the incumbent, and all three are indistinguishable from chance.
The stop rule says stop and report. This is the report.

Note what V *is* good at: seed spearman 0.808 and AUC(won) 0.767. It ranks runs
well and decision pairs not at all. That is not a contradiction — it is the
diagnosis. Every state in a run shares one label, so trajectories teach "how is
this run going" and contain nothing about "which of these two near-identical
portfolios is better". V learned exactly what it was trained on.

## 5. Why this is sample size, not architecture

The same 97 non-tied pairs, fit on the encoded *differences* `V(alt) − V(pick)`:

| model | sign rate |
|---|---|
GBM, in-sample | **0.887** |
ridge, in-sample | 0.804 |
GBM, **leave-seed-out** | **0.505** (49/97) |

Structure is present — a flexible model can express the mapping — and it does not
survive to held-out seeds. The pairs span 63 distinct item keys at ~1.5 pairs per
key across 86 seeds, so there is nothing to identify an item's effect from. This is
a data-volume verdict, and it is *actionable*: the S1 corpus is ~5× too small to
answer the question it was built to answer.

The second suspect is label noise. Iteration 2 collected with `--samples 1`: one
paired continuation per arm, so a ±1 label (the modal non-zero value) is one draw
of the draw-order lottery the K parameter exists to average out. If the labels
themselves are ~50% coin flips, then 0.505 with an in-sample 0.887 is exactly what
a correct model would show.

## 6. The run that tests that, and the stop rule it feeds

Running now: `--samples 3` over the same 500 seeds and the same focus work order,
so the decisions replay identically and the new pairs join the old ones.

```powershell
python tools/collect_decisions.py --seed-start 47200 --n-seeds 500 `
  --candidate-mode onpolicy --samples 3 `
  --focus-file results/coverage_focus_iter2.json --out-tag iter2k3 `
  --time-budget-min 70 --workers 12
```

It answers two questions at once, from data already being generated:

1. **Label noise.** `dense_samples` holds the K per-continuation deltas, so the
   split-half agreement of the K samples measures directly how much of a pair's
   sign is luck — no extra simulation.
2. **Volume.** The same 500 seeds yield ~3× the pairs, and averaging K
   continuations shrinks the label noise by ~1/√K.

Then, in order: refit; re-run this gate on the frozen pair set (train seeds) and on
holdout seeds; and only if the sign rate clears the incumbent's does any arm reach
a behavioural screen. If a fresh `--samples 3` corpus still cannot clear chance at
~500 non-tied pairs, the design doc's own conclusion applies — the decision lever
is dead at this granularity, and the honest next axis is the one §6 already named:
the *play/discard* decisions, where the same `V` can rank by one-step lookahead and
the labels are not near-ties by construction.

## 7. What would change the verdict

* **Split-half agreement near 1.0 across the 3 samples** → the labels are fine and
  the bottleneck is pure volume; scale collection and re-run S1.
* **Split-half agreement near 0.5** → the labels are noise; S1 as specified can
  never succeed and must be re-run on the K-averaged denser metrics (`ante_delta`,
  `label_win`) instead of the dense return.
* **S1 passes but a screen does not** → the pairs are learnable but not *causal*:
  the fork labels describe a decision the policy rarely faces, which is the
  off-policy drift risk §6 of the design doc already flags.

All artifacts uncommitted. Encoder, collector, fitter, gate and 7 new tests pass;
the previous suites are unaffected.
